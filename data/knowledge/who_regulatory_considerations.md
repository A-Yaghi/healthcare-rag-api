WHO's publication on regulatory considerations on artificial intelligence for health, developed with the ITU/WHO Focus Group on AI for Health Working Group on Regulatory Considerations, discusses key topic areas rather than prescribing a single binding regulatory framework. The six topic areas are: documentation and transparency; risk management and AI systems development lifecycle approaches; intended use and analytical and clinical validation; data quality; privacy and data protection; and engagement and collaboration.

Documentation and transparency: Developers should pre-specify and document intended medical purpose and development steps, including dataset selection, reference standards, parameters, metrics, deviations, and updates, so development can be traced. Documentation depth can follow a risk-based approach.

Risk management and lifecycle approaches: A total product lifecycle approach should cover pre-market development, post-market surveillance, and change management. Risk management should address cybersecurity, underfitting, algorithmic bias, and related AI-specific risks.

Intended use and validation: Intended use should be transparently documented. Training-dataset composition, including size, setting, population, inputs, outputs, and demographics, should be shared with users where appropriate. External analytical validation on an independent dataset representative of the deployment population is important. Clinical validation requirements may be graded by risk; randomized trials may be appropriate for the highest-risk tools.

Data quality: Available data should be good enough to support the intended purpose. Pre-release evaluations should check for amplified bias and errors. Stakeholders should work toward ecosystems that support sharing of good-quality health data.

Privacy and data protection: Privacy and data protection should be considered from design through deployment. Developers should understand applicable laws early and implement compliance programmes that address privacy, cybersecurity, harm, and enforcement context.

Engagement and collaboration: Accessible platforms for stakeholder engagement can help streamline oversight and accelerate responsible advances. International groups such as IMDRF and ICMRA are highlighted as forums for regulatory convergence discussions.

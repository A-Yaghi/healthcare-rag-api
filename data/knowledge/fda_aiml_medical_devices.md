In the United States, AI-enabled medical devices are regulated by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) under the medical device framework. Devices that include artificial intelligence or machine learning functions generally need to meet applicable premarket requirements focused on safety and effectiveness for the device's intended use and technological characteristics.

The FDA, together with Health Canada and the UK MHRA, published Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP) guiding principles for medical device development. These principles emphasize multidisciplinary expertise across the total product lifecycle, good software engineering and cybersecurity, representative training and test data, independence of training and test sets, clinically relevant testing, clear information for users, and monitoring of deployed models including management of retraining risks such as dataset drift.

The FDA also emphasizes transparency for machine learning-enabled medical devices. Transparency means communicating intended use, development information, performance, limitations, and, when available, model logic to relevant audiences such as clinicians, patients, caregivers, and other decision-makers. Clear intended use helps users understand whether a device is meant to inform or replace clinical judgment.

For iterative AI devices, the FDA has described predetermined change control plans (PCCPs) so manufacturers can describe planned modifications, validation methods, and impact assessments in a marketing submission. This U.S. pathway is device-centered and authorization-focused (for example 510(k), De Novo, or PMA pathways depending on the device), rather than a single horizontal AI law covering all sectors.

The FDA publishes an AI-Enabled Medical Device List to identify marketed AI-enabled devices authorized in the United States and to support transparency for innovators, healthcare providers, and patients. Inclusion is based primarily on AI-related terms in marketing authorization summaries and device classification information.

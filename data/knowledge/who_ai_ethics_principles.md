In 2021, WHO published guidance on the ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health. After consulting leading experts in AI ethics, digital technology, law, human rights, and ministries of health, WHO identified ethical challenges and risks and issued six consensus principles to help ensure AI works for the public benefit.

The six principles are: (1) protect autonomy; (2) promote human well-being, human safety and the public interest; (3) ensure transparency, explainability and intelligibility; (4) foster responsibility and accountability; (5) ensure inclusiveness and equity; and (6) promote AI that is responsive and sustainable.

These principles are intended to guide governments, public sector agencies, researchers, companies, and implementers in the development and deployment of AI in health care. WHO emphasizes that while AI can improve diagnosis, treatment, health research, drug development, and public health functions such as surveillance and outbreak response, ethics and human rights must remain at the heart of design, deployment, and use.

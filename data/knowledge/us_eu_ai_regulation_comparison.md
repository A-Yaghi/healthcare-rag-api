Are AI regulations in the United States and the European Union different for healthcare and medical imaging? Yes. The frameworks overlap in goals such as safety and transparency, but they are structured differently.

In the United States, AI used in medical devices is primarily handled through the FDA medical device regulatory system. Manufacturers typically seek FDA marketing authorization for a specific device intended use. Guidance and principles such as Good Machine Learning Practice, transparency for machine learning-enabled medical devices, and predetermined change control plans shape expectations, but there is not a single horizontal AI statute equivalent to the EU AI Act covering all sectors.

In the European Union, medical device AI generally remains under MDR or IVDR for product safety and conformity assessment, and the AI Act adds a horizontal risk-based layer. When a medical device AI system is a safety component or the product itself and requires notified-body third-party conformity assessment, it is typically treated as high-risk under the AI Act and must meet AI Act high-risk obligations in addition to MDR/IVDR duties.

Practical differences include: the U.S. pathway is centered on FDA device authorization and lifecycle controls for that device; the EU pathway combines sectoral medical device law with an across-the-board AI Act classification (prohibited, high-risk, transparency, or minimal risk). Both emphasize documentation, validation, post-market monitoring, and clear information for users, but the legal instruments and conformity routes differ.

Ionizing radiation has important applications in health care. It is used for diagnosis, including radiography and computed tomography; for image-guided procedures; in nuclear medicine; and for treatment, particularly radiotherapy.

When a procedure involving ionizing radiation is medically indicated and appropriately justified, its expected clinical benefit should outweigh the radiation risk. The exposure should then be optimized so that the medical purpose is achieved while exposure is appropriately managed.

Dose limits that apply to occupational and public exposure do not apply to the medical exposure of patients, because applying such limits could restrict the intended clinical benefit. Radiation protection of patients is therefore based particularly on justification and optimization. Patients who have concerns about a procedure should discuss expected benefits and potential risks with their health-care provider.

WHO also emphasizes communication of radiation risks in paediatric imaging. Children can be more vulnerable because of growing tissues and longer expected lifespan. When X-ray exams are needed for diagnosing illness or injury in a child and performed with proper technique, benefits can far outweigh radiation risks. Imaging that does not use ionizing radiation, such as ultrasound and MRI, may be alternatives in some situations. Radiology teams should use child-sized techniques and the smallest radiation amount needed for clinically useful images.

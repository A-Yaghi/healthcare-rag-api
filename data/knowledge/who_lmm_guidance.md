WHO guidance on ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health addresses large multi-modal models (LMMs), a form of generative AI that can accept one or more types of data input and generate diverse outputs not limited to the input modality. LMMs are sometimes called general-purpose foundation models. They can facilitate human-computer interaction that mimics human communication and may generate responses that appear authoritative.

Potential applications of LMMs in health care include diagnosis and clinical care support, patient-guided information and virtual assistance, clerical and administrative documentation tasks, medical and nursing education, scientific research, and public health uses. Potential benefits include helping manage complex cases, reducing documentation burden, generating explanations of medical conditions for patients or caregivers, drafting notes, and supporting translation.

Potential risks include inaccurate, incomplete, or false responses (including hallucinations); poor-quality or biased training data; automation bias; degradation of clinician skills; informed-consent and privacy concerns; reduced clinician-patient interaction; inconsistent outputs depending on prompts; and broader systemic risks around data-protection compliance, accountability, and concentration of power among large technology companies.

WHO states that its six ethical principles for AI for health also guide LMM governance: protect autonomy; promote human well-being, safety and the public interest; ensure transparency, explainability and intelligibility; foster responsibility and accountability; ensure inclusiveness and equity; and promote AI that is responsive and sustainable. The guidance is intended to help Member States map benefits and challenges and develop policies for appropriate development, provision, and use of LMMs.

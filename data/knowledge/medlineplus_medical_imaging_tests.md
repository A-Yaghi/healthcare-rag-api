According to MedlinePlus breast cancer and mammography topic summaries, health care providers may use several imaging and related tests when evaluating breast symptoms or screening for cancer.

A mammogram is an x-ray picture of the breast used to look for early signs of breast cancer that cannot be felt during a breast exam. Screening mammograms are used when there are no symptoms. Diagnostic mammograms take longer and are used when there is a lump or other signs, or when breast implants or other factors make tissue harder to view.

Other imaging approaches discussed in MedlinePlus-linked federal consumer materials include ultrasound and MRI as part of diagnosis pathways. A clinical breast exam checks for lumps or unusual findings in the breasts and armpits. If imaging or exams raise concern, a breast biopsy may be used to sample tissue. Blood chemistry tests may also be part of a broader workup.

If cancer is found, additional tests on the cancer cells such as HER2 testing and estrogen and progesterone receptor testing help guide treatment choices. Staging tests assess whether cancer has spread within the breast or elsewhere, sometimes including sentinel lymph node biopsy.

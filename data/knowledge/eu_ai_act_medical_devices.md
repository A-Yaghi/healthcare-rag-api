The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is a horizontal, risk-based law for AI systems placed on the EU market or put into service in the EU. It aims to address risks to health, safety, and fundamental rights while supporting innovation and a functioning internal market.

Unlike a purely medical-device authorization list, the AI Act classifies AI systems by risk. A limited set of uses is prohibited. High-risk AI systems face stricter obligations. Many systems remain minimal risk and can be used under existing law without extra AI Act obligations, aside from general applicable rules. Certain interactive or generative systems also have transparency duties.

High-risk AI systems include safety components of products covered by sectoral Union legislation, or AI systems that are such products themselves, when those products require third-party conformity assessment. Medical devices are an explicit example. High-risk systems can also include Annex III use cases such as systems that assess whether somebody can receive a certain medical treatment.

For medical device AI, the AI Act is intended to apply alongside the Medical Devices Regulation (MDR) and In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation (IVDR). MDR/IVDR already address medical device software risks, while the AI Act adds AI-specific requirements for health, safety, and fundamental rights. According to MDCG guidance on the interplay between MDR/IVDR and the AI Act, a medical device AI system is generally considered high-risk under Article 6(1) of the AI Act when it is a safety component or the device itself and it is subject to third-party conformity assessment by a notified body under MDR/IVDR. This commonly includes MDR Classes IIa, IIb, and III and comparable IVDR classes requiring notified-body involvement.

In short, EU regulation of AI in medical imaging or other medical devices often means simultaneous compliance with MDR/IVDR product rules and, where high-risk conditions are met, AI Act high-risk obligations.

Privacy and data protection should be considered during the design and deployment of AI systems for health. Early in the development process, developers should understand applicable data protection regulations and privacy laws and ensure the development process meets or exceeds those legal requirements. It is also important to implement a compliance programme that addresses risks and ensures privacy and cybersecurity practices account for potential harm as well as the enforcement environment.

Health AI systems should not treat medical or personal health data as freely reusable without legal basis, safeguards, and governance. Documentation and transparency expectations include tracing dataset selection and intended medical purpose. Data quality work should avoid amplifying bias and errors that can harm patients. Engagement with regulators and stakeholders is recommended as AI health systems mature.

In short, using AI on medical data is constrained by privacy law, cybersecurity, consent and lawful bases where required, intended-use documentation, and risk management. It is not an unrestricted free-for-all.
